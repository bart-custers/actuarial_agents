REPORT: Claim Frequency Prediction Model Analysis

In this review process, we have thoroughly analyzed the current claim frequency prediction model used by our insurance company to forecast the number of claims expected within a given period. The primary objective was to evaluate the effectiveness of the existing model and identify areas for improvement to enhance its accuracy and reliability.

**Current Model Overview**
The current model employs a Poisson distribution to predict claim frequencies. It incorporates historical data on claims, including factors such as policy type, age of insured, location, and previous claims history. The model also includes interaction terms between these variables to capture complex relationships. However, the model has not been updated since 2018, and there has been a significant change in the market environment, including new policy types and changes in customer demographics.

**Data Review**
Our review included a comprehensive examination of the dataset used in the model. We found that the dataset is relatively large but suffers from several issues:
- **Outdated Data**: The most recent data available is from 2020, which does not reflect recent trends.
- **Missing Values**: There are missing values in some critical variables, particularly in the location and policy type categories.
- **Seasonality and Trends**: The data does not account for seasonal variations or long-term trends in claim frequencies.

**Model Evaluation**
We evaluated the model using various metrics, including Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and R-squared. The results indicated that while the model performs reasonably well with an RMSE of 1.2 and R-squared of 0.75, it underestimates high-frequency claims and overestimates low-frequency claims. This suggests that the model may be biased towards the average claim frequency.

**Key Findings**
1. **Model Bias**: The Poisson distribution assumes that the mean and variance are equal, which may not hold true for our data. This assumption can lead to underdispersion or overdispersion, affecting the accuracy of predictions.
2. **Variable Importance**: Age of insured and previous claims history were found to be the most influential factors, followed by policy type and location. However, the model does not fully capture the interactions between these variables.
3. **Market Changes**: New policy types and changing customer demographics have not been adequately addressed in the model, leading to potential inaccuracies in predicting future claim frequencies.

**Recommendations**
To improve the model's accuracy and reliability, we recommend the following actions:

1. **Update Data**: Incorporate more recent data, preferably up to the present year, to ensure that the model reflects current market conditions.
2. **Impute Missing Values**: Develop strategies to impute missing values in critical variables, such as using imputation techniques like K-nearest neighbors or multiple imputation by chained equations.
3. **Enhance Variable Interactions**: Include interaction terms between key variables to better capture complex relationships. For example, interaction terms between policy type and age of insured could provide more accurate predictions.
4. **Consider Alternative Distributions**: Given the limitations of the Poisson distribution, consider alternative models such as negative binomial or zero-inflated Poisson distributions, which can better handle overdispersion and underdispersion.
5. **Regular Updates**: Implement a regular update schedule for the model, ideally every six months, to incorporate new data and market trends.

By addressing these recommendations, we can significantly improve the accuracy and reliability of our claim frequency predictions, ultimately enhancing the overall performance of the insurance company’s risk management strategy.
