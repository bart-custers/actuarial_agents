**REPORT: Claim Frequency Prediction Analysis**

**Introduction**

As part of our ongoing effort to improve the accuracy of our insurance claim predictions, we have conducted a comprehensive review of our existing data and methodologies. This report summarizes the key findings and recommendations from our analysis.

**Data Review**

We analyzed a dataset comprising 10,000 insurance claims over a 5-year period, covering various types of insurance policies, including auto, home, and health. The dataset included information on claim frequency, claim amount, policyholder demographics, and policy characteristics.

**Key Findings**

1.  **Claim Frequency Patterns**: Our analysis revealed a significant correlation between claim frequency and policy duration. Policies with shorter durations tend to have higher claim frequencies, indicating a higher likelihood of claims being made during the early stages of policy coverage.
2.  **Policyholder Demographics**: We found that policyholders with a history of previous claims are more likely to make future claims. This suggests that policyholder behavior and risk appetite play a crucial role in determining claim frequency.
3.  **Policy Characteristics**: The type of policy and coverage limits significantly impact claim frequency. For instance, policies with higher coverage limits tend to have lower claim frequencies, indicating that policyholders with higher coverage limits are less likely to make claims.
4.  **Seasonality**: Our analysis revealed a seasonal pattern in claim frequencies, with a peak in claims during the winter months (December to February) and a trough in the summer months (June to August).

**Model Development**

To develop a predictive model for claim frequency, we employed a generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) with a Poisson distribution. The model incorporated the following variables:

*   Policy duration
*   Policyholder demographics (age, sex, occupation)
*   Policy characteristics (type, coverage limits, deductible)
*   Seasonality

**Model Performance**

Our model achieved a high level of accuracy, with a mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 12.5% and a coefficient of determination (R-squared) of 0.85. The model's ability to predict claim frequencies was evaluated using a holdout sample, which consisted of 20% of the original dataset.

**Recommendations**

Based on our analysis and model performance, we recommend the following:

1.  **Targeted Underwriting**: Develop targeted underwriting strategies to identify high-risk policyholders and offer them more competitive premiums or policy terms.
2.  **Policyholder Engagement**: Implement initiatives to engage policyholders and encourage them to take proactive steps to reduce their claim risk, such as providing risk management tips and resources.
3.  **Seasonal Pricing**: Adjust premiums to reflect seasonal fluctuations in claim frequencies, with higher premiums during peak seasons and lower premiums during off-peak seasons.
4.  **Continuous Model Monitoring**: Regularly update and refine the predictive model to ensure its accuracy and relevance in predicting claim frequencies.

By implementing these recommendations, we aim to improve the accuracy of our claim frequency predictions and reduce the overall claim burden on the insurance company.
