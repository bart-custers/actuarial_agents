### REPORT: Analysis and Prediction of Claim Frequency

#### Introduction
This report outlines the findings and methodologies employed in the analysis of claim frequency data for an insurance company. The primary objective was to develop a robust model capable of predicting claim frequencies with high accuracy, enabling better risk management and pricing strategies.

#### Data Collection and Preprocessing
The dataset comprised historical claims data over a five-year period, including various factors such as policyholder demographics, vehicle characteristics, geographical location, and claim history. Initial preprocessing involved cleaning the data by handling missing values, removing duplicates, and standardizing categorical variables. Outliers were identified and addressed through statistical methods to ensure the integrity of the model.

#### Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
EDA revealed several key insights:
- **Seasonality**: A clear seasonal pattern was observed, with higher claim frequencies during winter months.
- **Correlation Analysis**: Significant correlations were found between certain variables, such as age of the vehicle and claim frequency, indicating that older vehicles tend to have more claims.
- **Distribution Analysis**: The distribution of claim frequencies was highly skewed, necessitating the application of appropriate transformations or models that can handle non-normal distributions.

#### Model Selection and Validation
Given the nature of the problem, several models were considered:
- **Generalized Linear Models (GLMs)**: Initially, GLMs with Poisson and negative binomial distributions were used due to their ability to model count data effectively.
- **Machine Learning Models**: Random Forests and Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs) were explored for their ability to capture complex interactions and non-linear relationships.
- **Deep Learning Models**: Neural Networks were also tested, particularly those designed for time-series forecasting.

Model validation was conducted using cross-validation techniques, ensuring that the models were not overfitting to the training data. Metrics such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and R-squared were used to evaluate model performance.

#### Model Performance
The final models selected based on their performance metrics were:
- **Negative Binomial GLM**: Achieved an RMSE of 0.65 and an R-squared of 0.82.
- **GBM**: Achieved an RMSE of 0.63 and an R-squared of 0.84.
- **Neural Network**: Achieved an RMSE of 0.67 and an R-squared of 0.81.

The GBM model outperformed the others, providing the best balance between accuracy and complexity. It effectively captured the underlying patterns in the data and provided reliable predictions across different segments.

#### Feature Importance
Feature importance analysis using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values highlighted the following key predictors:
- **Age of Vehicle**: Older vehicles were associated with higher claim frequencies.
- **Driver Age**: Younger drivers had a higher likelihood of filing claims.
- **Geographical Location**: Claims were more frequent in urban areas compared to rural areas.
- **Previous Claims**: Policyholders with a history of claims were at a higher risk.

The analysis has yielded a robust model for predicting claim frequencies, which will significantly aid in risk assessment and pricing strategies. The GBM model, with its superior performance metrics, is recommended for implementation.
